# Market Size, Economics, and Willingness to Pay: AI-Automation / AI-Tooling Creators

Scope note: research conducted 2026-09-28. Most figures come from third-party Skool trackers (revenuegeeks, codingoblin, skoolfiles, academygems, skoolmakers), which scrape Skool's public discovery pages. Member counts come from public pages. MRR figures are either (a) list price x member count estimates or (b) creator self-reports. They are labelled as such. None of these are audited. Skool, Whop and Gumroad do not publish category-level GMV.

## 1. How big is the AI-automation creator economy (Skool communities, key creators, Skool stats)?

### Takeaway
AI is the largest single category on Skool. About 13% of discoverable groups are AI-related, and 3 of the 5 largest communities are AI-automation funnels. The economy is extremely top-heavy. A handful of creators (Nate Herk, Nick Saraev, Jack Roberts, Liam Ottley, Duncan Rogoff) run huge free communities, often 100k-460k members, that feed paid tiers of roughly 2k-8.5k members at $69-$184/mo, for an estimated $200k-$500k+ MRR each. The median paid Skool group, by contrast, has about 200 members at $27-$39/mo, or roughly $5-8k/mo gross.

- The top 5-10 AI-automation creators probably gross about $2-4M/mo combined from Skool paid tiers alone, which is about $25-50M/yr. That is inferred from list price times members and is not audited.
- The creators' paid offer is mostly coaching, community, calls and templates, not software. The "proprietary logic" is usually n8n JSON, prompt files or Claude skills distributed as files. That is exactly the leak-prone asset a gated MCP server would protect.

- I found no audited revenue for any of the named creators. All MRR figures are list-price estimates or self-reports.

## 2. Revenue of template, prompt, and GPT sellers (n8n, Make/Zapier, Gumroad, PromptBase, GPT Store)

### Takeaway
Standalone template and prompt selling is a thin, power-law market. Top sellers make low thousands per month, and the median seller makes almost nothing. Real money flows to creators who bundle templates into a subscription community. n8n has no paid template marketplace; its creator program pays via a 30% affiliate commission and gives visibility.

### Cited Findings
- n8n: 30% commission on n8n cloud referrals for the first year. Creators can qualify via YouTube videos, blog posts, or templates in the n8n library. — [n8n affiliates](https://n8n.io/affiliates/)
- n8n Verified Creator program gives a badge, a private Discord and a directory listing, with no paid-template revenue share. — [n8n creators](https://n8n.io/creators/)
- The n8n community forum has users asking "where can I sell my n8n workflow... not the Creator Hub." This implies n8n's own hub is not a paid marketplace. — [n8n community](https://community.n8n.io/t/where-can-i-sell-my-n8n-workflow-i-am-looking-for-marketplaces-not-the-creator-hub/212963?tl=en)
- n8n template price points on Gumroad or personal sites run from $29 for simple workflows to $299+ for complex industry workflows. — [Ritz7 (search summary)](https://ritz7.ai/blog/monetize-n8n-automation-skills/)
- One anecdote reports "$47K last year selling the same 3 workflows" (self-reported and unverified). — [Medium / Rentier Digital (search summary)](https://medium.com/@rentierdigital/how-to-actually-sell-n8n-make-workflows-in-2025-and-stop-being-broke-5a88e5fe0beb)
- PromptBase: sellers keep 80%. Top-40 sellers reportedly clear $200-400/mo. Other sources say top sellers make $500-3,000/mo. PromptBase publishes no earnings distribution, and these figures are third-party anecdotes. — [Digiko](https://digiko.io/ai-money/selling-ai-prompts-is-a-25b-market-read-what-sellers-keep); [AI Profit Mode](https://aiprofitmode.com/how-to-sell-on-promptbase.html)
- GPT Store: one source claims payouts of about $0.03 per conversation and a 25-conversations/week floor. OpenAI has never publicly disclosed the formula (the US pilot was announced in early 2024), so treat the $0.03 figure as unverified. — [Digiko](https://digiko.io/ai-money/selling-ai-prompts-is-a-25b-market-read-what-sellers-keep); [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/gpt-store-custom-gpts-business-guide-2026)

### Inferences
- The GPT Store and PromptBase show that platform-native marketplaces with platform-controlled payouts produce very little creator income. Creators with audiences prefer to own billing (Skool, Stripe, Whop). The product should not rely on its own marketplace for distribution, at least at first.
- Template sellers' core problem is that a template is a copyable file. That supports the gated-MCP thesis. But the per-seller revenue of pure template sellers is too small to support a meaningful SaaS fee.

### Gaps
- There are no reliable public Make or Zapier template-seller statistics. Make's and Zapier's template programs do not publish payouts.
- Gumroad does not publish AI or prompt category sales.
- I found no public number for total n8n affiliate payouts.

## 3. Emerging Claude Code / Claude skills / MCP creator sellers (2025-2026)

### Takeaway
Paid Claude skills and MCP servers are real but early. Individual skills sell for $5-25 one-time, and the median listing earns under $50/mo. Top skill sellers report $500-3,000/mo, and one creator reports $10k+ cumulative. Paid MCP marketplaces exist (Apify, MCPize, MCP Marketplace, Agensi) at 70-85% creator revenue share. Most traction comes from Claude-Code-branded Skool communities that bundle skills and "OS" setups into memberships, not from standalone skill sales.

### Cited Findings
- Agensi (a curated SKILL.md marketplace) takes a 30% platform fee, so creators keep 70%. Top earners make "roughly $500-$3,000 per month." The median skill earns "less than $50 per month." Revenue is concentrated in "roughly the top 10% of listings." Individual skills sell for $5-25 one-time, with a sweet spot of $5-15. Published April 20, 2026. — [Agensi guide](https://www.agensi.io/learn/how-to-monetize-skill-md-skills-developer-guide-2026)
- "Subscription pricing for individual skills is experimental in 2026, with most buyers preferring one-time purchases because skills are simple files they download once." Skills at $1-2 "look unserious" and sell worse than $8-12 ones. — [Agent37 / Agensi (search summary)](https://www.agent37.com/blog/monetize-claude-code-skills)
- Ryan Doser's "Claude Code Skills Stack" has made over $10,000 since March 2026 as a one-time purchase, paid via Stripe. That is 25% of his income, with 75% coming from his agency. He attributes sales to SEO, YouTube and an email list. He mentions no piracy concerns. Published July 14, 2026 (self-reported). — [Ryan Doser](https://ryandoser.com/make-money-with-claude/)
- Claude Cowork plugins launched on January 30, 2026. Anthropic open-sourced 11 plugins, which bundle skills, connectors, slash commands and sub-agents. — [AI Blew My Mind](https://aiblewmymind.substack.com/p/claude-cowork-plugins-guide)
- MCP marketplaces:
  - MCP Marketplace pays creators 85% (15% fee) and ships license-key SDKs (Python and TypeScript) with key verification, caching, offline fallback and tier gating. Price points are $5-25 one-time or $5-50/mo. It gives no adoption or revenue numbers and calls the market "still early." Published March 3, 2026. — [MCP Marketplace](https://mcp-marketplace.io/blog/state-of-mcp-monetization-2026)
  - Apify gives developers 80%, with reported payouts of about $1.2M/month to developers across all Actors, not only MCP. — [MCPize / search summary](https://mcpize.com/blog/make-money-with-mcp)
  - MCPize gives 80% (85% for founding members before June 10, 2026). — [MCPize](https://mcpize.com/blog/make-money-with-mcp)
  - Document and knowledge-base MCP servers commonly charge $15-30/mo. A "realistic first-year target" is $500-5,000/mo, which is vendor-marketing language. — [MCPize](https://mcpize.com/blog/make-money-with-mcp); [Godberry Studios](https://godberrystudios.com/posts/how-to-monetize-mcp-servers-2026/)
  - Other billing infrastructure includes Nevermined, Stripe/x402, Moesif and MCP-Hive, which offer per-call, subscription, freemium and outcome-based pricing. — [Nevermined](https://nevermined.ai/blog/mcp-monetization-ai-agents); [ChatAds](https://www.getchatads.com/blog/tools-for-monetizing-mcp-servers/)
- AcademyGems' review of Claude Code Skool communities found no dedicated skill libraries, plugin packs or MCP servers sold as separate add-ons. Assets are bundled into membership (for example, Jack Roberts' "Claude Code and Hermes OS setup"). — [AcademyGems](https://academygems.com/best/claude-code-skool-communities)

### Inferences
- There are direct competitors on the MCP-gating mechanism: MCP Marketplace license-key SDKs, MCPize, Apify, and Nevermined/Stripe billing. None of them targets the creator-community use case, which means syncing access with Skool, Whop or Discord membership and revoking on churn. That is the likely wedge.
- Buyers prefer one-time purchases for file-based skills. A subscription-gated hosted skill must deliver ongoing value (updates, hosted execution, data) or it will face buyer resistance. The better framing is "access to the creator's skills is a membership perk that ends when membership ends," not "pay per skill."

### Gaps
- I found no public revenue from any named creator selling a subscription-gated, hosted MCP server to an audience.
- There is no count of paid skills or MCP listings across marketplaces.
- Anthropic has published no official paid plugin or skill marketplace or revenue share, as far as these searches found.

## 4. What creators pay for their stack today, and benchmark pricing and take rate for a new tool

### Takeaway
A typical AI-automation creator already pays about $99-$180/mo in platform fees plus about 3-10% of revenue in transaction fees. Established benchmarks: community SaaS costs $49-$179/mo; merchant-of-record or checkout fees run 3-10%; marketplaces with distribution take 15-30%. An add-on infrastructure tool with no distribution fits best at about $29-$149/mo or about 1-3% of gated revenue. A 15-30% marketplace take is only defensible if the tool also brings buyers.

### Cited Findings
- Skool: Hobby costs $9/mo (or $7.50 annual) plus 10% + 30¢ per transaction. Pro costs $99/mo (or $82 annual) with only Stripe processing (2.9% + 30¢), and adds a 1% platform fee on sales over $899. Break-even between the plans is about $1,300/mo of member payments. — [Skool pricing](https://www.skool.com/pricing); [Ruzuku](https://www.ruzuku.com/compare/skool-pricing); [RevenueGeeks pricing](https://revenuegeeks.com/software/skool/pricing)
- Whop: 3% for direct purchases plus processing of 2.7% + $0.30. Its marketplace fee was cut to 0% from 30%. Its blended take rate is about 5.5% (early 2025, estimated), up from 4.0% in 2022. — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/whop/)
- Gumroad charges 10% + $0.50 per sale, or 30% for Discover marketplace sales. It has been merchant of record since January 2025. — [Checkoutpage](https://checkoutpage.com/blog/gumroad-fees); [Swell](https://www.swell.is/content/gumroad-pricing)
- Lemon Squeezy charges 5% + $0.50, plus 1.5% international, 1.5% PayPal and 0.5% subscription surcharges. It is merchant of record with no monthly fee. — [Getly](https://www.getly.store/blog/gumroad-vs-lemon-squeezy); [WeAreFounders](https://www.wearefounders.uk/best-platforms-for-selling-digital-products-in-2026/)
- Kajabi Basic costs $143-$179/mo, plus 2.7-2.9% + $0.30 via Kajabi Payments. — [search summary: Kajabi pricing](https://checkthat.ai/brands/kajabi/pricing); [Cartmango](https://cartmango.com/kajabi-vs-circle/)
- Circle costs $49/mo Basic or $99/mo Professional, with a 4% transaction fee on the lowest tier. — [Circle vs Kajabi comparisons](https://www.ruzuku.com/learn/articles/kajabi-vs-circle); [Mihael Cacic](https://www.mihaelcacic.com/pricing-analysis/circle-so-pricing/)
- Skill and MCP marketplace fees: Agensi 30%, PromptBase 20%, Apify 20%, MCPize 15-20%, MCP Marketplace 15%. — [Agensi](https://www.agensi.io/learn/how-to-monetize-skill-md-skills-developer-guide-2026); [Digiko](https://digiko.io/ai-money/selling-ai-prompts-is-a-25b-market-read-what-sellers-keep); [MCP Marketplace](https://mcp-marketplace.io/blog/state-of-mcp-monetization-2026)
- Whop affiliate commissions are 30% recurring. n8n affiliate is 30% for year one. Creators are used to 30% as an affiliate or marketplace number. — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/whop/); [n8n](https://n8n.io/affiliates/)

### Inferences
- Stack cost: a mid-size AI creator on Skool Pro with a checkout tool and email tooling likely spends about $150-400/mo on software. The gating tool would be one line item among them.
- Pricing benchmark options:
  1. **Flat SaaS tiers**: about $29/mo for starters (at median Skool economics of $5-8k/mo gross, this is under 0.5% of revenue); about $99-149/mo for pro (roughly the Skool Pro and Circle Pro anchor); $299-499+/mo for top creators with 2k-8k members.
  2. **Per-active-subscriber pricing**: about $0.25-$1 per gated member per month. A 3,000-member community at $0.50 pays $1,500/mo. That is about 0.3-0.5% of a $99-184/mo tier, which is low enough to be painless and scales with value.
  3. **Take rate**: 1-3% of gated subscription revenue if the tool also does billing sync; 5% if it acts as merchant of record, which would replace Lemon Squeezy or Gumroad. Anything above about 5% would be stacked on top of Skool or Whop fees and would likely face pushback unless it brings demand.
- Top creators, with $200k-$500k MRR, would negotiate hard against any percentage-based fee. A capped fee or flat enterprise tier is likely needed. Pure take-rate pricing would put them at $2k-15k/mo, which at that level is well above what they pay Skool.

### Gaps
- I found no survey of creator willingness to pay specifically for IP protection or access revocation.
- I found no published price for competitors' creator-oriented MCP gating products beyond marketplace revenue shares.

## 5. Churn rates for paid communities and subscriptions

### Takeaway
Paid Skool communities typically churn about 10-18% per month. Sam Ovens reportedly calls 18% average and under 10% good. At that churn, a 3,000-member community loses about 300-540 members per month. Today each departing member can walk away with downloaded templates or skill files, which is the core revocation value proposition.

### Cited Findings
- "Average Skool community churn sits around 12-18% per month." Sam Ovens is cited as saying "18% is average, with less than 10% considered good." This comes second-hand via a search summary of Communipass and Skool community threads. I could not load the original Skool thread (it was login-walled or not rendered). — [Communipass churn guide](https://communipass.com/blog/how-to-reduce-churn-in-a-paid-community-12-retention-strategies-that-actually-work-in-2026/); [Skool "Average churn levels" thread](https://www.skool.com/educate/average-churn-levels)
- A broader 2026 benchmark puts paid-community churn at 5-10%/mo. It is not Skool-specific. — [Communipass (search summary)](https://communipass.com/blog/skool-revenue-benchmarks-2026/)
- "Healthy" Skool communities retain 65-75% month-over-month (a Communipass claim). Communities with leaderboards reportedly retain 70-80%, against 50-60% without. These figures are internally inconsistent with the 12-18% churn figure: 65-75% monthly retention implies 25-35% monthly churn, so the 65-75% may be 90-day retention. Low confidence. — [Communipass](https://communipass.com/blog/skool-revenue-per-member-2026/)
- Maker School churn is not disclosed. Retention is attributed to daily founder presence and a 90-day money-back guarantee. — [Skool Files](https://www.skoolfiles.com/blog/maker-school-nick-saraev-skool)

### Inferences
- Revocation value math (illustrative): at 3,000 members, $99/mo and 15% churn, about 450 members leave each month. If even 10-20% of them would keep using the creator's workflows or skills after leaving, a gated model could recover or protect about $4-9k/mo of value. More realistically, it deters "join, download everything, cancel" behaviour and so raises retention. A 1-point cut in monthly churn on a $300k MRR community is worth about $3k/mo, which comfortably justifies a $99-499/mo tool.
- High churn is a double-edged sword: it proves revocation value, but it also means the gated asset becomes a retention lever only if the MCP-served tools are used every week (sticky) rather than one-off.

### Gaps
- There is no primary, audited churn data for AI-automation communities specifically.
- There is no data on how often ex-members keep using downloaded templates or skills, which is the core quantity for revocation value.

## 6. TAM / SAM / SOM for "AI-tooling creators who would gate IP behind an MCP server"

### Takeaway
This is a niche but real market. Roughly 2,000-10,000 creators monetize AI-automation or AI-tooling content with some paid offer, generating on the order of $300M-$600M/yr in gross creator revenue, heavily concentrated in the top 20-50. The realistic TAM for a gating tool is about $5-25M ARR. SAM, meaning creators who distribute executable IP such as skills, templates or MCP, is about $2-10M ARR. A 3-year SOM is about $0.3-2M ARR unless the product expands beyond AI creators (for example to all Skool, Whop or course creators, or to B2B agencies licensing to clients).

### Cited Findings (inputs)
- 13% of Skool discoverable groups are AI-related (341 of 2,629). — [Codingoblin](https://codingoblin.com/blog/most-profitable-skool-community-niches)
- Skool has 10,000+ paying community owners (Latka estimate, low-confidence). — [GetLatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/skool.com)
- The median Skool group has about 200 members at $27-$39/mo, or about $5.5-7.8k/mo gross. — [RevenueGeeks](https://revenuegeeks.com/software/skool/statistics); [RevenueGeeks](https://revenuegeeks.com/software/skool/best-communities)
- Top AI creators have 2k-8.5k paid members at $69-$184/mo. See Section 1 table and [AcademyGems](https://academygems.com/best/claude-code-skool-communities).
- Whop has 183,628 sellers and a blended take rate of about 5.5%. — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/whop/)
- Skill marketplaces: median skill earns under $50/mo, and the top 10% earn $500-3k/mo. — [Agensi](https://www.agensi.io/learn/how-to-monetize-skill-md-skills-developer-guide-2026)

### Model (all assumptions explicit; these are my estimates, not sourced facts)

**Top-down: number of creators**
- A1: Paid Skool groups total 10,000-40,000. Latka's "10k+ paying owners" is the floor; the upper bound is a guess reflecting Skool's growth and the fact that the discovery page does not list all groups.
- A2: The AI share of paid groups is about 13%, using Codingoblin's share of discoverable groups. That gives **about 1,300-5,200 AI-focused paid Skool groups**.
- A3: Creators monetizing AI tooling off-Skool (Whop, Gumroad, Discord via Whop or Launchpass, own Stripe, Patreon, Circle, Kajabi, Agensi, MCP marketplaces) add 0.5x-1x more. That gives **about 2,000-10,000 AI-tooling creators with a paid offer**.
- Excluded: the long tail of skill or prompt sellers earning under $50/mo, who cannot pay a SaaS fee.

**Top-down: creator revenue pool**
- A4: Tail creators (all but the top 50) average about $5-8k/mo gross, based on the Skool median. 2,000-10,000 creators at about $6k/mo gives about $12-60M/mo, or **about $150-700M/yr**. The low end is more plausible, because the median is from a trending-list sample biased upward.
- A5: The top 20-50 AI creators average about $100-300k/mo, which gives about $2-10M/mo, or **about $25-120M/yr**.
- Combined gross creator revenue is roughly **$300-600M/yr** (midpoint about $400M). This is an order-of-magnitude figure.

**TAM (tool revenue if every AI-tooling creator adopted)**
- SaaS view: 2,000-10,000 creators at a blended $80-150/mo gives **about $2-18M ARR**.
- Take-rate view: 2-3% of $300-600M gives **about $6-18M ARR**.
- A hybrid of flat fee plus per-member charge, capped for the top tier, gives **about $5-25M ARR**.

**SAM (creators whose product includes executable IP they would gate)**
- A6: 30-50% of AI-tooling creators ship templates, skills, agents or "OS" setups as a core deliverable rather than pure coaching. AcademyGems' list suggests most leading ones do, such as n8n template libraries, blueprints and Claude Code setups, but many small groups are pure coaching.
- A7: The use case must fit MCP delivery. Buyers must be using Claude, Claude Code, Cursor or another MCP-capable client. That is increasingly true in 2026 as communities rebrand to Claude Code.
- SAM ≈ 30-50% × TAM ≈ **$2-10M ARR** (about 600-5,000 creators).

**SOM (3-year obtainable)**
- A8: 5-15% penetration of SAM gives about 100-750 paying creators.
- Bottom-up check: 100-750 creators × ARPA of about $100-200/mo, plus 5-15 top creators on enterprise deals of about $1-3k/mo, gives **about $0.2-2.3M ARR**.

**Bottom-up cross-check with named creators**
- Take the top ~10 named creators (Herk, Saraev, Ottley, Roberts, Rogoff, Finn, van Zyl, Catliff and others). They have about 25k paid members combined across paid tiers. At $0.50 per member per month, that is about $12.5k/mo, or about $150k ARR, from the top 10 alone. At a 2% take on their roughly $2-4M/mo MRR, it would be $40-80k/mo (about $0.5-1M ARR), but that is unlikely to be accepted.

### Inferences
- The creator count is small (thousands, not hundreds of thousands), so the business is a picks-and-shovels play with a ceiling in the low tens of millions of ARR, unless it:
  (a) extends to every creator who sells digital tools or templates (Notion, Figma, spreadsheets, trading bots and so on, with Whop's 183k sellers as the pool);
  (b) captures B2B flows, where agencies trained by these communities (Maker School and AAA Accelerator students) license automations to SMB clients and need revocable access. The number of agency operators is likely an order of magnitude larger than the number of creators; or
  (c) becomes the billing and merchant-of-record layer and takes 5%+.
- The best early customers are mid-tier creators with 300-3,000 paid members who ship Claude Code skills or "OS" bundles. They feel leakage pain, lack engineering teams, and are not big enough to negotiate custom deals.
- Distribution is concentrated. Winning 5-10 top creators (for example as affiliate or launch partners) would expose the product to hundreds of thousands of free-community members who aspire to be creators or agency owners. Those aspirants are the secondary market.

### Gaps
- The true count of paid Skool groups and the AI share of paid (not discoverable) groups are unknown.
- There is no category-level GMV for Whop or Gumroad AI products.
- There is no primary evidence of how many creators actually experience IP leakage, or their willingness to pay to stop it. This needs customer interviews or surveys.
- The share of community members who use MCP-capable clients is unknown and critical for SAM.

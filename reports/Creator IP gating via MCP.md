# Sell the running method, not the text

**Recommendation: conditional go.** Creators do have a real IP and revocation problem, but it is narrower, quieter and harder to solve than the pitch suggests. It hurts most for mid-tier sellers whose product *is* a downloadable file (n8n JSON, prompt packs, skill bundles) and for builders who deliver agents to paying clients. It barely touches the largest AI-automation creators, who give their templates away as marketing. The proposed architecture, a thin public plugin stub that fetches a versioned method from an authenticated "mission control" MCP server, runs into two problems. First, any method text returned into the model's context is disclosed to the paying user within one billing cycle: custom-GPT studies show 92–97% extraction rates. Second, Anthropic's Software Directory Policy forbids plugins that "dynamically pull behavioral instructions from external sources for Claude to execute," which is a plain description of a `get_skill` stub. Per-subscriber gating and automatic revocation are already commodities (Zuplo, MCPize, PayMCP, Stripe's `registerPaidTool`). What nobody sells yet is the combination of three things: entitlement synced to creator platforms (Skool, Whop, Stripe), delivery inside the buyer's own Claude or ChatGPT client, and proprietary steps that run on the server and return results instead of instructions. That combination is worth building as a focused infrastructure product for a small market, which the research estimates at $5–25M ARR in total. It should be positioned as "subscription access to a living, server-run method." It should not be pitched as DRM for prompts, and it should be released only after paid design-partner validation.

## The pain is real but concentrated in the middle tier

The most direct evidence comes from n8n sellers. In January 2026 a seller on the n8n forum asked how to stop a client reselling his workflow and wrote that "sending raw workflows + setup videos gives zero protection." He called the result a "ZIP file SaaS" that can simply be forwarded ([n8n Community](https://community.n8n.io/t/preventing-from-reselling-my-workflow/247365)). The replies in that thread show the prevailing attitude. One says "there's no such thing as absolute protection" and that sellers should either price as if they are handing over ownership or "expose functionality as an API" and sell access. That second option is the product's premise ([same thread](https://community.n8n.io/t/preventing-from-reselling-my-workflow/247365)). A separate 2025 thread proposing a platform to "sell n8n workflows without exposing the full blueprint" drew repeated "any updates?" replies. It also drew at least one team saying they were building "something exactly similar" ([n8n Community](https://community.n8n.io/t/idea-a-platform-to-sell-deploy-n8n-workflows-securely-without-sharing-entire-blueprints/138305)). So demand exists and is well understood, but it is thin, and rivals are already circling.

The strongest historical analogue is custom GPTs, where the prompt was the product and it ran on someone else's model. There the harm is well documented. Leaked-instruction repos such as friuns2/Leaked-GPTs (about 500 GPTs, about 2.5k stars) state outright that their purpose is to let people use GPTs "without a Plus subscription" ([GitHub](https://github.com/friuns2/Leaked-GPTs)). Wired-era coverage showed that simply asking for a GPT's files was often enough to download them ([Gold Penguin](https://goldpenguin.org/blog/custom-gpts-currently-let-anyone-download-context/)). Telegram channels advertise paid courses and "automation templates unlocked for free" ([TGStat](https://tgstat.com/channel/@paid_course_leak)).

Three pieces of evidence cut the other way, and they matter. First, the researchers found **no documented case of a third-party creator's paid Claude skill, plugin or MCP server being leaked**. The March 2026 "Claude Code leak" was Anthropic's own CLI source ([Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/tech/claude-code-leak-spawns-fastest-github-repo/)). Second, the largest operators do not treat files as the product. Nate Herk's AI Automation Society gives away 100+ n8n templates and charges for community and coaching; a review site reports roughly 452k free and 3.7k paid members ([AI Funnel Insider](https://aifunnelinsider.com/ai-automation-society-plus-skool-review/)). Free mega-collections of 2,600 to 16,000 workflows compete with paid packs regardless of piracy ([DEV](https://dev.to/vicckylove/i-put-together-16223-free-n8nworkflows-for-everyone-to-use-3djp)). Third, Ryan Doser reports more than $10,000 from a one-time Claude Code skills stack sold through Stripe and never mentions piracy ([Ryan Doser](https://ryandoser.com/make-money-with-claude/)).

The "download and dip" pattern, where members join a community, download everything and cancel, is mechanically plausible but lacks direct testimony. Skool members lose access at the end of the billing cycle and keep whatever they downloaded ([Skool Help](https://help.skool.com/article/99-how-to-cancel-my-subscription-to-a-community)). Scrapers can bulk-export Skool classrooms ([Apify](https://apify.com/crustapi/skool-content-scraper)). Secondary sources put Skool churn at **12–18% per month**, with "60% of churners" leaving in week one ([Communipass](https://communipass.com/blog/how-to-reduce-churn-in-a-paid-community-12-retention-strategies-that-actually-work-in-2026/)). But no community owner was found saying members download templates and leave. One important caveat applies throughout: Reddit, X and Discord were unreachable during this research, so the absence of complaints there reflects a coverage gap, not evidence that the complaints do not exist.

The pain therefore sorts as follows. It is highest for workflow builders delivering to clients (the resale fear) and for paid communities whose main deliverable is a downloadable library. It is moderate for skill and prompt sellers, who tend to accept the risk. It is lowest for top creators, whose templates work as lead magnets. The more compelling pitch is **"access ends when payment ends, and the method keeps improving"**, not "stop leaks." The first runs with how creators already think. The second runs into their fatalism and, as the next section shows, into what is technically possible.

## Serving method text into context protects almost nothing after one fetch

Anything an MCP tool returns into Claude's context should be treated as handed to the paying user. A Northwestern study of 216 custom GPTs found **97.2% system-prompt extraction and 100% file leakage**, and experts bypassed every "do not reveal your instructions" defense within ten attempts ([arXiv 2311.11538v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.11538v2)). A 2025 study of **14,904 GPTs found 92.2% vulnerable** to prompt leakage ([arXiv 2505.08148](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.08148)). OWASP's 2025 LLM Top 10 says a system prompt "should not be considered a secret, nor should it be used as a security control" ([OWASP LLM07](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm07-insecure-plugin-design/)).

MCP in Claude Code adds leak paths that need no model cooperation at all. Every session is stored locally as plain JSONL under `~/.claude/projects/` ([Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/claude-directory)). Tool results over the size cap are written to a file on the buyer's disk ([Claude Code MCP docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/mcp)). The OAuth token lives in the client, so a buyer can point any generic MCP client or a short script at the server and call `get_skill` directly. The server cannot tell that script apart from a legitimate agent. And even without the text, prompt-stealing research reconstructs commercial prompts from PromptBase and the GPT Store using only input–output examples ([USENIX Security '25 PRSA](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/usenixsecurity25-yang-yong.pdf); [arXiv 2402.12959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12959)).

So revocation protects three things: future updates, work done on the server, and convenience. It does not recover a static method that has already been delivered. For a static method, one paid month is enough to capture all of it. Protection only holds for what never leaves the server:

| Pattern | What leaks | Revocation value | Directory-policy fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monolithic `get_skill` returns the full method | Everything, on first call | Updates only | Conflicts with the policy |
| Many small, context-dependent fetches | Everything, with more effort | Updates only | Conflicts with the policy |
| Hybrid: readable public skeleton, with proprietary scoring, rubrics, templates and data lookups run on the server | Outputs only (which can be approximately reverse-engineered) | Strong | Fits |
| Full server-side execution with proprietary data, per-customer state and integrations | Outputs only | Strongest | Fits |

Per-customer fingerprints (a different but equivalent set of examples or constants for each tenant) and audit logs help identify who leaked something and support a ToS or DMCA claim. They do not prevent the leak. Literal canary strings are defeated by paraphrase ([DEV](https://dev.to/yohannsidot/how-canary-tokens-detect-system-prompt-leaks-in-real-time-74p); [arXiv 2506.19109](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.19109)).

The upside is that curated methods are genuinely valuable and cannot simply be regenerated by the model. SkillsBench found curated skills raised agent pass rates by **16.2 percentage points** on average (up to 51.9pp in healthcare), while self-generated skills gave no benefit on average ([arXiv 2602.12670](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12670)). The method is worth protecting. It just cannot be protected as text.

## Anthropic's own rules forbid the stub-fetches-instructions mechanic

Anthropic's Software Directory Policy states that "Instructional Software must not direct Claude to dynamically pull behavioral instructions from external sources for Claude to execute." It also bans "hidden, obfuscated, or encoded instructions," requiring that "all behavioral guidance must be human-readable" ([Anthropic Software Directory Policy](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/13145358-anthropic-software-directory-policy)). On a plain reading, a public stub whose job is to fetch its method from Mission Control and follow it is exactly what the first clause prohibits. The second clause rules out obfuscating the stub as a workaround. This applies directly to the aipathway-co/plugins repo. `PUBLISHING.md` treats community-marketplace listing as an optional path, but the current `ai-staff` design would probably not pass directory review. It is unclear how strictly reviewers enforce the clause and whether it reaches self-hosted marketplaces; the policy appears to govern directory listings only. Off-directory distribution (`/plugin marketplace add`, custom connector URLs) still works, at a cost. It gives up discovery. On Claude Team and Enterprise only an Owner can add a custom connector. Anthropic labels custom connectors "not verified by Anthropic." Free users are limited to one custom connector ([Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11175166-get-started-with-custom-connectors-using-remote-mcp)).

The policy points toward the same fix that the leakage analysis does. Tools that do real work on the server and return *results* fit both the policy and the protection model. A tool that returns *instructions* for Claude to execute fails both.

Platform risk is rising. Claude Marketplace launched on **September 23, 2026** with more than 2,000 connectors and plugins. It lets enterprises spend part of their committed Anthropic budget on partner products such as Cursor, Harvey and Snowflake ([Claude blog](https://claude.com/blog/claude-marketplace); [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/artificial-intelligence/anthropic-turns-claude-into-an-ai-marketplace-with-2-000-plus-plugins-and-connectors/)). A submission portal followed around September 25 ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/claude-plugin-mcp-connector-submission-portal/)). No primary source shows paid listings or revenue share for individual creators. A widely repeated claim of a May 2026 "Skills Marketplace" with a 15% cut cites no Anthropic announcement and should be treated as unverified ([500k.io](https://500k.io/journal/anthropic-skills-marketplace-launch)). The direction is still clear. If Anthropic adds per-seat plugin licensing, a standalone "turn off the tap" billing layer becomes a platform feature.

OpenAI is more compatible with this model but restricts in-app selling. Its plugins may not sell "subscriptions, digital content, tokens, or credits," but users "may sign in to an existing paid account" ([OpenAI submission guidelines](https://developers.openai.com/apps-sdk/app-submission-guidelines)). External billing plus OAuth is therefore the only legitimate model there, which is exactly this product's design. The same server could support a ChatGPT app with no changes to how billing works.

## Gating is commoditized; the gap is creator-native entitlement plus server-side execution

Per-subscriber keys and automatic revocation are solved problems. Zuplo issues plan-scoped keys when a customer subscribes and "revokes access automatically when a payment goes overdue," including for MCP traffic ([Zuplo](https://zuplo.com/features/api-monetization)). MCPize issues "one API key per subscriber" and pays developers 80% ([MCPize](https://mcpize.com/developers)). PayMCP adds `@subscription` decorators to tools ([GitHub](https://github.com/PayMCP/paymcp)). Stripe's agent toolkit checks subscription status before running a paid tool ([DeepWiki](https://deepwiki.com/stripe/ai/3-model-context-protocol-(mcp)-server)). Polar with xmcp validates license keys on each call ([xmcp](https://xmcp.dev/blog/polar-integration)). The closest competitors on IP protection host execution themselves. Agent37 runs Claude skills on its own servers so "customers never download or see the skill files," starting at $3.99/month ([Agent37](https://www.agent37.com/blog/monetize-claude-code-skills)). Pickaxe hosts creator chatbots behind Stripe subscriptions ([Pickaxe](https://pickaxe.co/pricing)).

| Player | Target | Auto-revoke | IP hidden | Runs in buyer's own Claude | Community entitlement sync |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zuplo | API/enterprise teams | Yes | Gateway only | Via MCP | No |
| MCPize | MCP developers | Via keys | Hosted | Via MCP | No |
| PayMCP / Stripe / Polar SDKs | Developers | Yes | Depends on host | Via MCP | No |
| Agent37 | Skill creators | Presumably | Yes | No (hosted workspace) | No |
| Pickaxe | Non-technical creators | Yes | Yes | No (chat UI) | No |
| Agensi | Skill creators | Catalog tier | Partly | Partly ($9/mo MCP tier) | No |
| Claude Marketplace | Enterprises, vetted partners | N/A | No (plugins copied locally) | Yes | No |

Sources: [Zuplo](https://zuplo.com/features/api-monetization), [MCPize](https://mcpize.com/developers), [PayMCP](https://github.com/PayMCP/paymcp), [Agent37](https://www.agent37.com/blog/monetize-claude-code-skills), [Pickaxe](https://pickaxe.co/pricing), [Agensi](https://www.agensi.io/learn/best-ai-agent-skills-marketplaces-2026), [Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugin-marketplaces).

No surveyed product combines creator-audience focus, hidden IP, execution inside the buyer's own client, and entitlement from Skool or Whop. Skool is the clearest opening. It has no public API; "the only official programmatic interface is Stripe webhooks for payment events," plus Zapier's "Invite Member" and "Unlock Course" actions ([RevenueGeeks](https://revenuegeeks.com/software/skool/api)). Reliable "revoke on Skool churn" is therefore real integration work that the developer-oriented tools skip. The most dangerous fast follower is Whop. It already runs MCP servers and a Claude Code plugin, automates Discord and Telegram role gating, and has ample capital ([Whop docs](https://docs.whop.com/developer/guides/ai_and_mcp); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/whop/)). Agent37 and Agensi could add MCP-served, per-buyer entitlement quickly.

## A small market that rewards a focused infrastructure play

This is a niche. AI is the largest category on Skool, with 13% of discoverable groups ([Codingoblin](https://codingoblin.com/blog/most-profitable-skool-community-niches)), but the median paid group has about 200 members at $27–39/month ([RevenueGeeks](https://revenuegeeks.com/software/skool/statistics)). Top AI creators run 2,000–8,500 paid members at $69–184/month ([AcademyGems](https://academygems.com/best/claude-code-skool-communities)). The research team's model estimates 2,000–10,000 AI-tooling creators with a paid offer. That gives a **TAM of about $5–25M ARR**, a SAM of about $2–10M among creators who ship executable IP, and a realistic 3-year SOM of **$0.3–2M ARR**. These are assumption-driven estimates, not measured figures. Standalone skill sales are too small to support a SaaS fee: the median Agensi skill earns under $50/month ([Agensi](https://www.agensi.io/learn/how-to-monetize-skill-md-skills-developer-guide-2026)). Buyers also prefer one-time purchases for file-based skills. That makes subscription gating credible only as a membership perk or a living service.

The timing is favorable. Community momentum shifted in 2026 from n8n to Claude Code. Duncan Rogoff's Claude Code Club has about 8,500 members, and Jack Roberts bundles a "Claude Code and Hermes OS setup" into his membership ([AcademyGems](https://academygems.com/best/claude-code-skool-communities)). None of these communities yet sells skill libraries or MCP servers as separate gated add-ons. The retention case is simple arithmetic. At 15% monthly churn, a 3,000-member community loses about 450 members a month. A one-point churn reduction on $300k MRR is worth about $3k/month, which easily covers a $99–499 tool. That only holds if members use the server-run tools every week.

Creators already pay Skool Pro $99/month plus Stripe processing ([Skool pricing](https://www.skool.com/pricing)) and Whop a blended ~5.5% ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/whop/)). A layer that brings no new buyers cannot charge marketplace-style 15–30%. **Pricing guidance:** a flat Starter tier around $29/month; Pro at $99–149/month, anchored to Skool Pro and Circle; and Scale at $299–499/month for communities of 2,000–8,000 members. Alternatively, charge $0.25–1.00 per active gated member per month with a cap. Keep any percentage fee at or below 1–3% of gated revenue, and use capped enterprise deals for top creators, who would reject uncapped percentages. Treat compute-heavy server-side execution as metered usage, because the strongest protection pattern also carries the highest cost of goods.

## Recommendation: conditional go, with explicit build and kill criteria

**Go if** three conditions hold within about 90 days. First, at least 3 of 5 design partners each pay $99/month or more before general release. Second, those partners accept restructuring their method into server-side tools that return results, rather than serving it as fetchable text. Third, the team accepts distribution outside Anthropic's directory, or gets written clarification that a results-returning design is listable. **No-go if** partners only want "hide my prompt file," or if Anthropic or Whop announces native per-seat plugin entitlement before traction exists.

**What to build:** Entitlement sync from Stripe, Whop and Skool (via the owner's Stripe webhooks and Zapier), with Discord roles as a secondary source. A multi-tenant OAuth 2.1 MCP gateway following the 2025-11-25 spec with CIMD support, so that one server serves Claude Code, Claude.ai connectors and ChatGPT ([MCP spec](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/draft/basic/authorization)). A framework that helps creators convert a method into server-side tools: scoring, rubrics, template filling, data lookups and per-customer memory, with a readable public skeleton that satisfies the human-readability rule. Per-customer audit logs, rate limiting and detection of anyone enumerating every branch of a method. Per-tenant structural fingerprints for attributing leaks. Versioned method publishing with rollback. The AI Pathway Mission Control stack already has much of this (config, audit, versioned methods). The main architectural change is moving from `get_skill`-returns-instructions to tools-return-results.

**What not to build:** DRM or obfuscation marketing. A marketplace or discovery layer; the platform-native marketplaces studied here (the GPT Store, PromptBase) paid creators very little. Merchant-of-record billing at launch. x402 or crypto per-call payments, which do not match how audiences pay (monthly subscriptions). A hosted chat workspace that competes with Agent37 and Pickaxe on their ground.

**Beachhead:** mid-tier Claude Code creators with 300–3,000 paid members whose deliverable is a skill library or "OS" bundle. They feel the leakage and churn pain, have no engineering team, and are too small to negotiate custom terms. Recruit them through one or two top creators acting as launch or affiliate partners. Run a parallel design-partner lane with agencies that license agents to SMB clients. This is where first-person resale pain is clearest, and it is the model AI Pathway already runs.

**Key risks, in order:** (a) method exfiltration by paying users, which is unsolvable for text and must be designed around with server-side execution; (b) Anthropic directory non-compliance for the stub-fetches-instructions pattern, together with Claude Marketplace later adding native paid licensing; (c) Whop, Agent37 or Agensi moving first into creator-native MCP gating; (d) a small market with top-heavy buyers who resist percentage fees; and (e) unproven willingness to pay, since no survey or interview data exists on what creators would spend on revocation.

## Conclusion

The research changes the product's definition. The original assumption was that secrecy of the method is the asset and gating protects it. The evidence says secrecy fails within one billing cycle, and Anthropic's rules forbid the mechanism that would attempt it. What can be defended is a method that keeps running on the creator's server, keeps improving, and stops working when payment stops. That product protects creators better, complies with platform policy, and fits OpenAI's external-billing rules. It also puts the company in the business of hosting execution rather than storing text. That brings real compute costs, deeper creator onboarding, and a moat built on integrations and workflow rather than a vault.

For AI Pathway specifically, the question of whether to productize comes after a more urgent one: the current `ai-staff` stub-plus-`get_skill` design leaks its own method to any customer who asks Claude for it, and would likely fail Anthropic directory review. Fixing that for AI Pathway's own agents is the first design-partner project. It is also the most convincing demo to show creators.
